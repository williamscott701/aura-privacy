2. Not medical advice, not a medical device

Aura is a diary. It is not a medical device, it does not diagnose anything, and it does not give medical advice. The developer is not a clinician. Nothing the app displays is a clinical assessment of you, and no doctor-patient relationship is created by using it.

Aura records what you tell it and reports patterns in what you recorded. It does not diagnose migraine or any other condition, does not distinguish migraine from any other kind of headache, gives no guidance about medicines or doses, and is not a substitute for a clinician. Nothing in it should ever be a reason to start, stop or change a treatment.

The pattern analysis reports association, not causation. A finding that “attack days went with less sleep” means those two things appeared together in what you wrote down. It does not mean sleep caused your migraines, that the factor is your trigger, or that changing it will change anything. Correlations in a self-reported diary can arise from coincidence, from recall, from a third cause affecting both, and from the way an attack itself changes the day around it. Take a finding to your clinician as an observation, not a conclusion.

One figure in the app is drawn from outside your diary: the count of days with acute medication is shown against the threshold clinical guidance commonly cites for a month. Aura reports that such guidance exists and that the figure may be worth raising at an appointment. It is not a limit set for you, not an instruction, and not a judgement about your treatment.

3. Emergencies

Aura is not for use in an emergency. Some headaches need urgent attention rather than a diary entry: one that reaches full intensity within about a minute or is the worst you have ever had; one with fever, a stiff neck or a rash; one after a head injury; one with confusion, seizure, fainting, weakness, loss of vision or difficulty speaking that does not settle as your usual aura does; a first severe headache after 50; or a headache in pregnancy with swelling or visual changes. Contact your emergency number or urgent care. Do not open an app.

That list is general published safety information reproduced for convenience. It is not advice about your circumstances, it is not exhaustive, and it does not replace contacting a clinician about anything that worries you.

11. Accuracy and your responsibility

Everything Aura shows is derived from what you typed. An attack logged on the wrong day, a severity chosen while you could barely look at the screen, a check-in filled in from memory a week later, or a stretch of days left blank will all produce a wrong answer, and the app cannot detect any of it. Findings drawn from a small number of days are weak evidence even when the arithmetic is right, which is why Aura always shows you how many days each one rests on.

Reminders depend on iOS delivering notifications, which can be delayed, silenced by Focus modes, or dropped when the system is under pressure. Do not rely on Aura as your only reminder for anything that matters, and never as a reminder to take medication — it does not offer one, and it must not be used as one.

This device holds the only copy of your diary. Keeping a backup — an export, or an iPhone or iCloud backup — is your responsibility, and the developer cannot recover data that has been lost.
